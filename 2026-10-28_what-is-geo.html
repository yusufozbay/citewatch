H2: How Generative Engines Work: RAG and Query Fan-Out

Goal: Fill the technical gap exposed by the Google Developers snippet in Snapshot 1.

Extractable Summary Block (52 words)

Generative engines do not generate search responses solely from static pre-training weights; they rely on Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). When a user asks a complex question, the system breaks it down into multiple sub-queries—a process called query fan-out—retrieves relevant web content chunks via vector embeddings, evaluates source authority, and synthesizes an answer.

H2: The Ecosystem: GEO vs. AEO vs. LLMO vs. AI SEO

Goal: Resolve nomenclature confusion shown across snapshots 2, 3, 5, and 6.

  • AEO (Answer Engine Optimization): Historically focused on featured snippets, voice search (Alexa, Google Assistant), and direct Q&A boxes.
  • LLMO (Large Language Model Optimization): Optimizing to ensure an entity or brand is embedded within the core weights and training corpora of foundation models during pre-training.
  • GEO (Generative Engine Optimization): The overarching modern discipline targeting dynamic, real-time RAG systems across AI search engines.
  • AI SEO: Generic umbrella term often conflated with using AI tools to produce traditional SEO copy.

H3: Is traditional SEO dead because of GEO?

No. Traditional SEO forms the baseline index that generative engines crawl to extract real-time content for RAG. Without strong crawlability, indexability, site speed, and domain authority, your content will never enter the retrieval pool from which AI answers are generated.

H3: What platforms are targeted in GEO?

GEO targets AI search engines and conversational response tools. Major platforms include Google AI Overviews and Google AI Mode, Perplexity AI, ChatGPT (SearchGPT), Claude, Microsoft Copilot, and Google Gemini.

H3: What is the fastest way to get content cited in AI Overviews?

The fastest path is answering specific user queries directly in 40–60 words immediately following an H2 or H3 tag (BLUF style), backing the answer with verified statistics and named sources, implementing valid Schema markup, and currently ranking within Google's top 10 search results for the underlying query.

H3: How does Schema markup help GEO?

Schema markup provides machine-readable context that eliminates ambiguity. By utilizing JSON-LD structured data to clearly label entities, authors, definitions, and data points, you allow LLMs to parse relationships and verify facts without relying strictly on probabilistic text interpretation.
